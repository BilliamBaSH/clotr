What Clotr can reach

    
Nothing outside your browser. Clotr has no server and makes no connections. Your browser enforces this
      rule on Clotr's own pages (read live from the installed copy):

    

    
connect-src 'self' means these pages can only load Clotr's own files: no website, no
      server. The part that watches AI chat boxes runs inside those pages; it contains no code that sends anything, and an
      automated check fails the build if any is ever added.

    
What Clotr is allowed to do, and why

    

    
Check it yourself

    
    
      
  • Open brave://extensions (or chrome://extensions), turn on Developer mode, and under Clotr
        click service worker. In the window that opens, the Network tab stays empty while you use AI chats.

  • Every release zip can be rebuilt from the published code with npm run package: the result is
        byte-for-byte the same, so its checksum matches the one in the release's SHA256SUMS.txt.

Known limits

  
    
    
  • Short details can be guessed from their fingerprints by someone who can open this browser's
      profile on this computer: there aren't many possible phone numbers, so a determined person could try them all.
      Fingerprints protect your details from casual reading, not from someone with full access to your computer.
    • 
    
  • AI sites may keep your unsent message in this browser. Some (like ChatGPT) save what you're typing
      so it's still there after a reload; nothing is sent until you press Send. Hiding something replaces the saved copy too.
    • 
    
  • Clotr only reads text. It checks what you type and attached text, PDF, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, but not pictures, screenshots or voice. If your vault has details, it also reads the AI's reply for 90 seconds after you send, only to notice your own details; nothing from a reply is kept.
    • 
    
  • Clotr only works on AI chat sites it knows or that you added, and only in this browser.
    • 
    
  • It can miss things and sometimes warn about ordinary text. It's a safety net, not a guarantee.
