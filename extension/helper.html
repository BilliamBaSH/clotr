Clotr

Set Clotr up for someone

For a parent, a grandparent or a child. Four steps and a few minutes, on their computer, in the browser they use for AI chats.

Managed by your organization. Clotr's settings on this computer are set by your IT team. It still only checks AI chats, on this computer, and reports nothing to anyone.

Settings are locked

Someone who set Clotr up for you added a PIN, so settings aren't changed by accident. Clotr keeps protecting you either way.

  1. Tell Clotr what to protect

    Their name, phone numbers, home address, email and the names of family members. Clotr keeps only one-way fingerprints of them, never the details themselves.

  2. Make the warnings bigger

    Bigger text and bigger buttons in Clotr's notes, easier to read and to click.

  3. Ask before personal details go out

    When a message holds a phone number, an address or a birthday, Clotr stops and asks before it's sent. Passwords and keys always get a warning.

  4. Lock the settings with a PIN

    The PIN stops accidental changes. It isn't a real lock, since anyone who can remove Clotr from the browser can reset it.

What they'll see

A note in the corner of the AI chat when their message has something private in it, with a button to hide it. With step 3 on, Clotr asks before a personal detail goes out. Clotr itself never sends anything anywhere.
